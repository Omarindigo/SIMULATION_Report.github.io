1. Abstract

This report documents the training of a humanoid locomotion agent using Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) on the MuJoCo physics engine via Gymnasium's Humanoid-v4 environment. The project began with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) but transitioned to SAC due to its superior sample efficiency, GPU utilization, and exploration characteristics. A total of 800,000 timesteps of training were completed, with checkpoints saved every 25,000 steps. Four key checkpoints (200K, 400K, 600K, 800K) were rendered at 1920 × 1080 resolution to visualise the progression of gait quality — from early-stage stumbling to stable, fluid locomotion at the final checkpoint. This page serves as both a report and a visual record of that progression.

2. Methodology

The project began with PPO but migrated to SAC. The transition was driven by five factors:

Sample Efficiency

SAC is off-policy. It stores every experience in a 1M-capacity replay buffer and reuses them for multiple gradient updates. PPO discards data after one update pass. Result: SAC learns to walk in far fewer environment steps with the same GPU budget.

GPU Utilization

SAC repeatedly samples training batches from the replay buffer and performs dense tensor ops, keeping the GPU saturated. PPO's collect-once, update-once cycle is more CPU-bound and underutilises GPU throughput.

Exploration

SAC incorporates entropy maximisation directly into its objective function. The agent is rewarded for maintaining uncertainty, driving wide exploration of the action space. PPO relies on fixed noise schedules that can converge to local optima prematurely.

Continuous Control

SAC's twin-Q architecture mitigates value overestimation bias, a known failure mode in continuous control. This is critical for Humanoid's 17-degree-of-freedom action space. PPO's clipped surrogate objective is simpler but empirically less effective for high-DoF locomotion.

Empirical Results

PPO Humanoid-v5 plateaued at 250K timesteps with marginal walking quality. SAC Humanoid-v4 scaled to 800K with measurably more fluid gait at every checkpoint — from rough stumbling at 200K to stable walking at 800K.

SAC is an off-policy actor-critic algorithm with three architectural innovations:

Actor (Policy Network)

Maps observations to action distributions. Updated to maximise both expected return and entropy, balancing exploitation with exploration.

Twin Critics (Q₁, Q₂)

Two Q-networks reduce overestimation bias by taking the minimum of their outputs. Target networks use soft Polyak averaging (τ = 0.005) for stable learning.

Entropy Regularisation

Temperature α is learned automatically. High α drives exploration early; lower α narrows the policy as gait skill improves.

Replay Buffer

FIFO buffer of 1,000,000 transitions (s, a, r, s′). Batches of 256 are sampled uniformly for each gradient step. Enables repeated reuse of past experience.

4. Discussion

The progression from Figure 1 to Figure 4 demonstrates a clear trajectory of improvement. At 200K timesteps, the agent exhibits uncoordinated joint movements and frequent loss of balance. By 400K, basic stability emerges — the agent can maintain an upright posture for sustained periods. At 600K, a walking gait pattern becomes recognisable, and by 800K, locomotion is consistent and energy-efficient.

Several factors contributed to this outcome. The off-policy nature of SAC allowed efficient reuse of experience, which is particularly valuable in high-dimensional continuous control tasks. The entropy regularisation term ensured the agent continued exploring alternative gait strategies rather than converging to a suboptimal pattern early in training. The replay buffer size of 1M transitions provided sufficient diversity in the training distribution.

The transition from PPO to SAC proved critical. PPO's on-policy constraint limited the effective use of GPU parallelism, and its fixed exploration schedule was insufficient for the 17-DoF Humanoid action space. SAC's twin-Q critics addressed the value overestimation bias that would have caused PPO to overestimate the return of untested state-action pairs.
